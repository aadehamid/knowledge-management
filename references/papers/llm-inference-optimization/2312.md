# Paper page - LLM in a flash: Efficient Large Language Model Inference with Limited Memory

## Abstract

Efficient inference for large language models on devices with limited DRAM by optimizing data transfer and access from flash memory.

[Large language models (LLMs)](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=Large%20language%20models%20%28LLMs%29) are central to modern natural language processing, delivering exceptional performance in various tasks. However, their intensive computational and memory requirements present challenges, especially for devices with limited [DRAM](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=DRAM) capacity. This paper tackles the challenge of efficiently running LLMs that exceed the available [DRAM](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=DRAM) capacity by storing the model parameters on [flash memory](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=flash%20memory) but bringing them on demand to [DRAM](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=DRAM). Our method involves constructing an [inference cost model](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=inference%20cost%20model) that harmonizes with the [flash memory](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=flash%20memory) behavior, guiding us to optimize in two critical areas: reducing the volume of [data transfer](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=data%20transfer)red from flash and reading data in larger, more contiguous chunks. Within this [flash memory](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=flash%20memory)-informed framework, we introduce two principal techniques. First, "windowing'" strategically reduces data transfer by reusing previously activated neurons, and second, "row-column bundling", tailored to the sequential data access strengths of [flash memory](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=flash%20memory), increases the size of data chunks read from [flash memory](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=flash%20memory). These methods collectively enable running models up to twice the size of the available [DRAM](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=DRAM), with a 4-5x and 20-25x increase in inference speed compared to naive loading approaches in CPU and GPU, respectively. Our integration of [sparsity awareness](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=sparsity%20awareness), [context-adaptive loading](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=context-adaptive%20loading), and a [hardware-oriented design](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=hardware-oriented%20design) paves the way for effective inference of LLMs on devices with limited memory.

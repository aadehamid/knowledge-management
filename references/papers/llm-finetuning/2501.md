# SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes: A Comparative Study of Foundation Model Post-training

## Abstract

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) are widely used post-training techniques for foundation models. However, their roles in enhancing model generalization capabilities remain unclear. This paper studies the difference between SFT and RL on generalization and memorization, focusing on text-based rule variants and visual variants. We introduce GeneralPoints, an arithmetic reasoning card game, and adopt V-IRL, a real-world navigation environment, to assess how models trained with SFT and RL generalize to unseen variants in both textual and visual domains. We show that RL, especially when trained with an outcome-based reward, generalizes across both rule-based textual and visual variants. SFT, in contrast, tends to memorize training data and struggles to generalize out-of-distribution scenarios. Further analysis reveals that RL improves the model's underlying visual recognition capabilities, contributing to its enhanced generalization in the visual domain. Despite RL's superior generalization, we show that SFT remains essential for effective RL training; SFT stabilizes the model's output format, enabling subsequent RL to achieve its performance gains. These findings demonstrates the capability of RL for acquiring generalizable knowledge in complex, multi-modal tasks.

## AI Overview

## Comparative Analysis of Post-Training Methods {#comparative-analysis-of-post-training-methods}

This research investigates a fundamental question in foundation model development: how do different post-training approaches affect a model's ability to generalize versus memorize? The study provides a systematic comparison between Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) across both language-only and vision-language tasks, revealing distinct learning characteristics that have significant implications for AI system design.

![Training Performance Comparison](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures/2501.17161v2/img-0.jpeg "Training Performance Comparison")

The core finding challenges conventional assumptions about post-training methods. While both SFT and RL can improve in-distribution performance, they exhibit fundamentally different behaviors when models encounter novel situations that require applying learned principles to unseen contexts.

## Experimental Framework and Methodology {#experimental-framework-and-methodology}

The research employs a carefully controlled experimental design using the Llama-3.2-Vision-11B model as the foundation. The authors developed two distinct evaluation environments that isolate different aspects of generalization:

**GeneralPoints (GP)**: A card game requiring arithmetic reasoning where models must create equations using four given cards to reach a target number (typically 24). This environment tests rule-based generalization through variations in how face cards are interpreted - training with one rule (J, Q, K \= 10) and testing with another (J\=11, Q\=12, K\=13).

**V-IRL**: A real-world navigation task where models follow instructions to reach destinations using visual landmarks. This environment evaluates spatial reasoning generalization by varying action spaces (absolute vs. relative directions) and visual contexts (different cities).

![Task Examples](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures/2501.17161v2/img-1.jpeg "Task Examples")

Each environment includes both language-only variants (GP-L, V-IRL-L) and vision-language variants (GP-VL, V-IRL-VL), enabling comprehensive analysis across modalities. The RL framework incorporates a multi-turn setting with a verifier component that provides outcome-based rewards and textual feedback, allowing for iterative self-correction.

The training methodology follows established practices where models are first initialized with SFT for basic instruction following, then subjected to either additional SFT or RL training. This design enables direct comparison of the incremental effects of each post-training approach.

## Core Findings: Generalization vs. Memorization {#core-findings-generalization-vs-memorization}

The results demonstrate a clear dichotomy between SFT and RL in their effects on model behavior:

**RL Consistently Promotes Generalization**: Across all tested environments, RL training improved out-of-distribution performance. In language-only tasks, RL achieved \+3.5% improvement in GP-L and \+11.0% in V-IRL-L. Even in the more complex vision-language settings, RL maintained these generalization benefits with \+3.0% in GP-VL and \+9.3% in V-IRL-VL.

**SFT Tends Toward Memorization**: In stark contrast, SFT consistently degraded out-of-distribution performance across all tasks. The effects were particularly pronounced in navigation tasks, with SFT showing -79.5% degradation in V-IRL-L and -33.2% in V-IRL-VL when encountering novel rule variations.

![Performance Comparison](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures/2501.17161v2/img-3.jpeg "Performance Comparison")

This pattern extends beyond rule-based variations to visual generalization. When trained on cards with black suits and tested on red suits, or trained on New York City navigation and tested on worldwide cities, RL maintained its generalization advantage while SFT showed decreased performance.

## Multi-Modal Insights and Visual Capabilities {#multi-modal-insights-and-visual-capabilities}

The study reveals particularly interesting dynamics in vision-language models. RL training not only improves task-level generalization but also enhances underlying visual recognition capabilities. Analysis of the GP-VL task showed that RL improved the model's ability to correctly identify card values from images, suggesting that outcome-based rewards can refine perceptual processing beyond what supervised training achieves.

![Visual Performance Analysis](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures/2501.17161v2/img-6.jpeg "Visual Performance Analysis")

This finding is significant because it indicates that RL's benefits extend beyond high-level reasoning to fundamental perceptual abilities. The structured reward system in RL appears to encourage the model to develop more robust visual recognition patterns that generalize across different visual contexts.

## The Role of SFT in RL Training {#the-role-of-sft-in-rl-training}

While the results favor RL for generalization, the study clarifies that SFT plays a crucial enabling role. Direct application of RL to the base model without prior SFT failed completely, as the foundation model lacked basic instruction-following capabilities necessary for meaningful task engagement.

$$
v_{t}^{in}=\text{concat}(v_{0}^{in},[v_{k}^{out},v_{k}^{ver}]_{k=0}^{t-1})
$$

The multi-turn RL framework constructs inputs by concatenating the initial prompt with all previous model outputs and verifier feedback, enabling iterative refinement. However, this process requires the model to produce structured, task-relevant outputs - a capability that SFT provides.

This suggests an optimal training paradigm where SFT serves as a "format teacher," establishing basic instruction-following and output structure, while RL acts as a "generalization enhancer," promoting transferable knowledge acquisition.

## Inference-Time Computation and Verification {#inference-time-computation-and-verification}

The research demonstrates that scaling inference-time computation through multiple verification steps significantly enhances RL's generalization benefits. Increasing verification iterations from 1 to 10 steps yielded \+5.99% improvement in out-of-distribution performance for GP-L, compared to only \+0.48% for a single verification step.

![Verification Steps Impact](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures/2501.17161v2/img-8.jpeg "Verification Steps Impact")

This finding aligns with recent trends emphasizing inference-time scaling and suggests that RL's iterative self-correction mechanism becomes increasingly valuable with more computational resources during deployment.

## Implications for Foundation Model Development {#implications-for-foundation-model-development}

These results have profound implications for foundation model training strategies. For applications requiring robust generalization to novel rules, contexts, or visual environments - such as autonomous systems, scientific reasoning, or adaptive problem-solving - RL should be prioritized in the post-training pipeline.

The research also provides guidance for resource allocation in model development. While SFT remains essential for establishing basic capabilities, investing additional computational resources in RL training appears more beneficial for achieving generalization than extended SFT.

Furthermore, the finding that RL can improve underlying visual capabilities suggests potential applications beyond task-specific training, pointing toward more general approaches for enhancing multimodal foundation models' perceptual robustness.

The work establishes a clear empirical foundation for understanding how different post-training methods shape model behavior, moving beyond intuitive assumptions to provide concrete evidence for designing more capable and reliable AI systems.

Thanks for the questions. From a high level perspective, our key message is indeed somewhat similar to deepseek that "RL is beneficial". However, in large model training, the devil is always in the details. Several technical difference and my own conjectures are that:

Difference: Deepseek R1 tech report mentioned in their abstract that they do not require sft before running RL, while we report that SFT is still necessary for stable RL training. See more discussion in section 5.4 of our paper.

Conjecture: We conjecture that SFT is required for models that are not "good enough", in the sense that the model cannot answer the question (could due to bad instruction following, or bad visual capability, or other reasons. In our case, we found that llama3.2 has decent visual capability for our tasks, but bad in instruction following, so we have to do SFT first so that llama3.2 can generate legit outputs for the rewards. **Note that this observation is highly dependent on the backbone model we are using, it could be the case that SFT is not necessary for RL to work, if the backbone model is good enough.**

Thought: I think it would be beneficial for whoever is interested in this phenomenon to try more open-sourced models (such as Qwen2.5 vl) or others, so that to verify whether this phenomenon still holds for other models. We spent like 6 months on this project, our compute budget cannot afford ablation studies on another backbone model unfortunately.

So in conclusion, we conjecture that whether SFT is necessary as a cold start for RL is highly task & model dependent, we would like to see more evidence on the necessity of SFT. In llama3.2, we observed that "decent amount or SFT is good for downstream RL" (section 5.4 & figure 19 suggest that RL fails without SFT and too much SFT), but this could potentially be a llama-only phenomenon.
